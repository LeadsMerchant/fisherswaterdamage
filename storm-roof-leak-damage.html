Storm & Roof Leak Water Damage

Indiana storms bring wind, heavy rain, and sometimes hail, often all at once. When that water gets inside, we can connect you with an independent provider who handles the ceilings, walls, and floors.

Spring and summer thunderstorms are part of life in central Indiana. Strong straight-line winds can lift shingles and snap branches onto roofs, and the heavy rain that follows finds every gap. Many neighborhoods in Fishers, especially older ones and those near Geist and the White River, have large mature trees close to the house.

How storm water usually gets in

  • Wind-lifted or missing shingles, and damaged flashing around chimneys and vents
  • Tree limbs puncturing the roof or knocking gutters loose
  • Hail cracking shingles or skylights
  • Gutters clogged with leaves and seed pods overflowing behind the fascia
  • Wind-driven rain past window and door seals
  • Downspouts dumping next to the foundation, which can show up later as basement water

Ice dams in winter

When snow melts on a warm roof and refreezes at the cold edge, it can form an ice dam that backs water up under the shingles. The leak often shows up as a stain along an exterior wall or ceiling in the middle of winter, with no rain in sight.

Two jobs: outside and inside

A roofer repairs the roof and may tarp it in the meantime. A restoration provider deals with what already got in: wet attic insulation, ceiling drywall, framing, and anything below. If a tree is on the house, you may also need a tree service before anyone can work safely.

After the storm

  1. Keep people out from under any ceiling that is sagging with water.
  2. Turn off power to leaking light fixtures and fans.
  3. Photograph outside damage (shingles in the yard, dented gutters, fallen limbs) and inside damage.
  4. Be cautious with door-to-door contractors after big storms, and check out any company before you sign.
  5. Call {{PHONE}} to be connected with a provider for the inside water damage.

Quick answers

Do you repair roofs?

No. We are a referral service for the water damage inside the home. You will need a roofer for the roof itself.

Should I wait for the adjuster before drying?

Insurers generally expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. Take photos, then start drying. Check your own policy for details.
