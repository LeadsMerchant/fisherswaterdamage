Water Damage Restoration in Fishers

When an appliance or plumbing line lets go, the water heads for the lowest point, which in most Fishers homes is the basement. Our free line connects you with independent providers who extract the water and dry the structure.

Fishers grew quickly over the past few decades, and many homes from that growth now have original water heaters, washing machine hoses, toilet supply lines, and dishwashers reaching the end of their life. A second-floor laundry room is convenient until a hose bursts and the water comes through the kitchen ceiling below.

Common sources of indoor water damage

  • Water heaters leaking or failing, often in the basement or a utility closet
  • Washing machine hoses, especially in upstairs laundry rooms
  • Refrigerator ice maker lines and dishwasher connections
  • Toilet supply lines and wax ring leaks
  • Water softener and filter connections
  • Shower pans and tub surrounds leaking into the ceiling below

What restoration usually involves

Independent restoration providers typically confirm the source is stopped, then use moisture meters and often thermal cameras to find where the water went. Standing water is extracted, materials that can’t be saved (swollen cabinet bases, wet pad, soaked drywall) are removed, and drying equipment runs until readings return to normal. Most also keep photos and moisture logs, which insurers commonly ask for.

Water travels down and sideways

In a two-story home with a basement, one leak can affect three levels: the room where it started, the ceiling below, and the basement under that. Water also wicks sideways through drywall and under flooring. That is why a provider may want to check rooms that look fine.

What to do right now

  1. Turn off the water at the fixture or the main shutoff, usually in the basement near the water meter or where the line enters the house.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas if you can reach the panel safely.
  3. Move rugs, furniture, and electronics off wet floors.
  4. Take photos and video before cleanup starts.
  5. Call {{PHONE}} to be connected with a local restoration provider.

If the water came from a frozen pipe, see burst and frozen pipes. If it ended up in the basement, see flooded basement cleanup.

Quick answers

Can I dry it myself with fans?

Fans move surface moisture but often miss water trapped in walls, pad, and subfloors. Professional moisture readings show whether materials are actually dry.

Who fixes the leak itself?

Usually a licensed plumber or appliance technician. The restoration provider handles the water damage. Ask who does what so nothing falls through the cracks.
