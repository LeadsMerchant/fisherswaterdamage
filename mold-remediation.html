Mold Remediation After Water Damage

Indiana’s humid summers and damp basements give mold plenty of chances. If you smell mustiness or see growth after water damage, we can connect you with an independent mold remediation provider.

Mold needs moisture, something to grow on, and a little time. A finished basement offers all three: concrete that wicks ground moisture, carpet and drywall to feed on, and summer humidity that keeps things damp. Add a sump pump failure or a slow leak, and growth can start behind the drywall before anyone notices.

Where mold commonly shows up

  • Finished basements after a flood or seepage, especially behind drywall and under carpet
  • Basement storage rooms and closets with poor air movement
  • Bathrooms without a working exhaust fan
  • Attics after a roof leak or with poor ventilation
  • Under kitchen and bathroom sinks with a slow drip

What remediation generally involves

Independent providers usually begin with the moisture source, because mold comes back if the water does. Depending on the size of the area, they may set up containment and air filtration, remove affected porous materials like drywall, insulation, and carpet, clean and treat framing and hard surfaces, and dry everything before walls go back up. Some homeowners choose independent testing before or after the work.

Questions worth asking

  • What is the moisture source, and is it fixed?
  • How will you contain the work area?
  • What will be removed versus cleaned?
  • What training or certifications does your crew have (for example, IICRC)?
  • How will you confirm the area is dry before rebuilding?

Keep the basement dry

  • Run a dehumidifier in the basement during humid months.
  • Keep the sump pump tested and backed up.
  • Point downspouts well away from the foundation.
  • Don’t store cardboard boxes directly on the basement floor.
  • Dry any spill or seep within a day or two.

What we will not claim

We do not diagnose health conditions, guarantee a “mold-free” home, or interpret lab results. Talk to a medical professional about health concerns. We connect you with a provider who can assess the home.

Quick answers

When should I call a professional?

U.S. EPA guidance suggests professional help for areas larger than about 10 square feet, for mold in HVAC systems, or for mold tied to sewage or contaminated water.

Will a dehumidifier get rid of mold?

A dehumidifier helps prevent mold by lowering humidity, but it won’t remove existing growth. Affected materials still need to be cleaned or removed.
